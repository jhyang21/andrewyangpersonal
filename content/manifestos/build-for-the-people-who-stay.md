This is the thesis that changed the most. It started as a confident conviction and ended somewhere I didn't expect.

## The original belief

At immForm, the power users drove everything. They filed the best bug reports, pushed for advanced features, and were the loudest advocates. I built the mental model that power users are your real customers — everyone else is noise.

When I started Relora, I carried that belief with me. Build for the people who use it the most. Depth of engagement is the signal.

## The first crack

During the Relora beta, something strange happened. Our most active users — the ones recording 5+ notes a day — rarely complained about the onboarding flow. They'd figured it out through brute force. But new users and casual users kept getting stuck at the same three points.

If I'd only listened to power users, I would have missed these problems entirely. They had adapted around the friction. The product wasn't good — they were just persistent.

## The data that broke the thesis

In January 2026, I pulled retention cohorts and the numbers told a story I wasn't ready for. Our highest lifetime value users weren't the power users recording notes daily. They were the casual users who recorded maybe one note a week.

These people weren't engaged by any traditional metric. But they kept coming back. Week after week, month after month. They trusted Relora with their small, important moments. A birthday to remember. A detail about a colleague. A note before a catch-up call.

The power users, meanwhile, had high engagement but high churn. They'd burn bright and move on to the next productivity tool. The casual users just stayed.

## What I believe now

Build for the people who stay, regardless of how they use your product. Power users are loud and helpful — listen to them for bug reports and feature ideas. But don't confuse volume of usage with value of relationship.

The quiet ones who keep coming back once a week — they're your real foundation. They're not trying to optimize their workflow. They're just trying to be a little more thoughtful in their relationships. And they trust you to help with that.

Retention isn't about depth of usage. It's about whether someone trusts you enough to come back.
