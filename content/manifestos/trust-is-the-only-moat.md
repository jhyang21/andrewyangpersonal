When I started building Relora, I obsessed over features. Voice-to-note transcription, smart reminders, contact enrichment, conversation starters. I thought the product with the most capabilities would win.

Then a competitor launched something almost identical. Same core interaction — tap to record, AI structures the note. My first reaction was panic. If features are the game, someone with more resources will always catch up.

But something unexpected happened: none of our active users left. Not one.

## Features are borrowed time

Any feature you build can be replicated. API wrappers, UI patterns, even novel interactions — they all get copied eventually. The half-life of a feature advantage is shrinking every year. With AI tooling, it might be weeks.

This isn't a reason to stop building features. It's a reason to stop thinking of features as your moat.

## What trust actually means in a product

Trust isn't a marketing word. In the context of a daily-use product, trust means:

- I believe this app won't lose my data
- I believe this app respects my privacy
- I believe this app will still be here next month
- I believe the people building this care about the same things I care about

Trust is earned through consistency, not spectacle. It compounds quietly. Every time someone opens Relora and it just works, every time their notes are exactly where they left them, every time a reminder surfaces at the right moment — that's trust accruing.

## The switching cost of trust

When someone trusts your product, they stop evaluating alternatives. They don't compare feature lists. They don't read competitor reviews. They just stay.

This is the moat. It's invisible from the outside, but it's the strongest force in consumer products. The cost of switching isn't the effort of migrating data — it's the emotional cost of starting over with something unproven.

## What this means for how I build

I now evaluate every product decision through the lens of trust, not features. Will this make people trust us more or less? Does this feel reliable? Does this feel like it was built by someone who cares?

The moat isn't what you build. It's what people believe about what you build.
